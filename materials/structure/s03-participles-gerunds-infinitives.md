# S03 — Participles, Gerunds, Infinitives

**Section:** Structure & Written Expression · **Est. yield:** 4–6 questions
**Time to learn:** 25 min

---

## Three forms, three jobs

| Form | Looks like | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Present participle** | doing / writing | continuous verb · active modifier · adjective (*causing* the feeling) |
| **Past participle** | done / written | passive · perfect · adjective (*feeling* the effect) |
| **Gerund** | doing / writing | a **verb acting as a noun** |
| **Infinitive** | to do / to write | purpose · after certain verbs · after adjectives |

---

## 1. -ed vs -ing adjectives — the classic trap

> **-ed** = the person/thing *feels* it. **-ing** = the thing *causes* the feeling.

```
The lecture was boring.   → The students were bored.
The news was shocking.    → We were shocked.
The result was surprising. → Nobody was more surprised than I.
```
Test: substitute *"which makes people feel ___"* → use **-ing**.

---

## 2. Participle phrases as modifiers
```
The scientist ___ the experiment received an award.
(A) conducting  (B) conducted  (C) who conducting  (D) was conducting
```
→ **A**. Active, ongoing → *conducting*. If the scientist *received* the action you'd use *conducted*.

⚠️ **Dangling modifier** (a favourite Written Expression error):
```
❌ Walking down the street, the building looked enormous.   (the building isn't walking)
✔ Walking down the street, I thought the building looked enormous.
```

## 3. Gerund or infinitive after a verb?

**Verb + GERUND** (no exception list needed — these are common):
`admit, appreciate, avoid, consider, delay, deny, discuss, enjoy, finish, keep, mind, miss,
postpone, practise, quit, recall, recommend, resist, risk, suggest, understand, can't help,
give up, look forward to, be used to, object to, be accustomed to`

**Verb + INFINITIVE:**
`agree, aim, appear, arrange, ask, attempt, choose, dare, decide, demand, deserve, expect,
fail, hope, learn, manage, offer, plan, prepare, promise, refuse, seem, tend, threaten, want, wish`

**Either, meaning-change:**
| Verb | Gerund | Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| remember | recalling a past event | not forgetting to do something later |
| stop | quitting an activity | pausing in order to do something |
| try | experimenting | making an effort |
| regret | sorry about the past | sorry to announce (formal) |

**Preposition → always gerund:**
`before, after, without, by, in, for, of, about, despite, in spite of, instead of, look forward to`
```
✔ He succeeded by working carefully.     ❌ …by to work carefully.
```

---

## 4. Purpose = infinitive
```
The committee met ___ the budget.   → to review   (NOT for reviewing)
```

## 5. Adjective + infinitive
```
It is important ___ the rules.      → to follow
The question is easy ___ .          → to answer
too difficult ___ / old enough ___  → to + base verb
```

## 6. Causatives
| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `have / get + object + past participle` | arrange for someone else to do it |
| `make / let + object + base verb` | force / allow |
| `have + person + base verb` | cause someone to do it |

```
She had her paper ___ .             → proofread   (someone else did it)
The coach made them ___ extra laps. → run         (base verb, no to)
```

## 7. Verbs of perception
`see / hear / watch / notice + object + base verb` (whole action) **or** `+ -ing` (in progress).

## Exercise — 12 items, 7 minutes

1. The audience found the documentary thoroughly ___ .
   (A) fascinated  (B) fascinating  (C) fascinate  (D) fascination
2. She avoided ___ the question directly.
   (A) answer  (B) to answer  (C) answering  (D) answered
3. The professor had the manuscripts ___ by a graduate assistant.
   (A) organize  (B) organizing  (C) organized  (D) to organize
4. ___ in the 19th century, the building is now a museum.
   (A) Constructing  (B) Constructed  (C) Having constructing  (D) To construct
5. They decided ___ the meeting until next week.
   (A) postponing  (B) postpone  (C) to postpone  (D) postponed
6. He is looking forward to ___ his family again.
   (A) see  (B) seeing  (C) saw  (D) be seen
7. The instructions were too complicated ___ .
   (A) for following  (B) to follow  (C) following  (D) that we follow
8. ___ carefully, the experiment can yield reliable results.
   (A) Doing  (B) If doing  (C) Done  (D) Having done
9. I clearly remember ___ her at the conference last year.
   (A) to meet  (B) meeting  (C) meet  (D) to have meet
10. The dean made the department ___ its budget proposal.
    (A) revise  (B) to revise  (C) revising  (D) revised
11. The results were so surprising that no one expected ___ them.
    (A) see  (B) seeing  (C) to see  (D) saw
12. ___ finished the report, she turned off the computer and went home.
    (A) Having  (B) Have  (C) To have  (D) Being
