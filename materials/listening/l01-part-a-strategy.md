# L01 — Part A: Short Conversations (30 questions)

**Section:** Listening Comprehension · **Est. yield:** 30 of the 50 listening questions
**Time to learn:** 30 min

Part A is 30 short dialogues: two speakers, two lines each, one question. About **half** of
them do not test facts at all — they test **what the speaker is doing**.

---

## The 6 question types

| # | Type | What it asks | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Function / idiom** | What does the speaker *mean*? | The reply doesn't literally answer the question |
| 2 | **Inference** | What is probably true? | *What can be inferred / What does the man imply* |
| 3 | **Location** | Where are they? | *Where does this conversation take place* |
| 4 | **Occupation / relationship** | Who are they? | *Who is the woman / What is the man's job* |
| 5 | **Suggestion** | What should be done? | *What does the woman suggest* |
| 6 | **Detail / number / sequence** | A stated fact | *How much / When / What time* |

---

## The master rule for type 1

> **If the second speaker's reply does not literally answer the question, stop asking
> "what does it mean?" and start asking "what is this person DOING?"**

```
W: Did you finish the lab report?
M: Are you kidding? I've only just started the research.
Q: What does the man mean?
→ He is a long way from finishing. He is NOT answering yes/no; he's expressing "far from it".
```

**Speaker functions to name:** agreeing · disagreeing · refusing politely · suggesting ·
surprised · annoyed · hedging/uncertain · encouraging · apologising · correcting ·
impressed · dismissive · sarcastic

## The second-speaker patterns

| Pattern | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Agree strongly** | full agreement | *I couldn't agree more.* / *You can say that again.* / *I'll say!* |
| **Disagree softly** | polite no | *I see your point, but…* / *That may be true, however…* |
| **Refuse** | no | *I'd love to, but…* / *I'm afraid I can't.* / *Thanks, but no thanks.* |
| **Not decided** | neutral | *I haven't made up my mind.* / *I'm still on the fence.* |
| **Surprised** | unexpected | *You're kidding!* / *No way!* / *I had no idea.* |
| **Certain** | confident | *Without a doubt.* / *I'm positive.* / *It's a sure thing.* |
| **Uncertain** | hedging | *I'm not so sure.* / *More or less.* / *Sort of.* |
| **Suggestion** | advice | *Why not…?* / *How about…?* / *If I were you, I'd…* / *You might try…* |
| **Leave it to you** | your choice | *It's up to you.* / *Whatever you think.* / *Suit yourself.* |
| **Not possible** | impossible | *That's out of the question.* / *No way.* / *Fat chance.* |
| **Easy** | simple | *It's a piece of cake.* / *No sweat.* / *It's a breeze.* |
| **Rushed** | no time | *I'm in a hurry.* / *I've got to run.* / *I'm pressed for time.* |

## The negative-meaning trap

English hides negatives in positive-looking sentences. About 3–5 Part A questions per test:

| Heard | Actually means |
|---|---|
| *I could hardly hear her.* | I almost couldn't hear her. |
| *The lecture was anything but boring.* | It was NOT boring. |
| *She is the last person I'd ask.* | I would NOT ask her. |
| *He is far from ready.* | He is NOT ready. |
| *It's beyond me.* | I don't understand. |
| *I rarely skip class.* | I almost never skip. |
| *That's the last thing I need.* | I definitely don't need it. |
| *She seldom agrees.* | She almost never agrees. |
| *I couldn't care less.* | I don't care at all. |
| *Not in the least.* | Absolutely not. |

---

## Technique

1. **Predict before the audio.** In the ~8 seconds before each dialogue, read nothing (in the real
   test you can't see the choices) — instead set your attention: *Who are they? Where? What's the relationship?*
2. **Listen to the second speaker harder.** 80% of answers live in line 2.
3. **Catch the tone.** Rising = surprise or doubt. Falling flat = bored or certain. Slow = emphasis.
4. **Let go instantly.** If you miss one, guess, bubble it, move to the next. Chasing a lost item
   costs you three more.
5. **Never change an answer.** Your first instinct on audio is correct more often than your second guess.
