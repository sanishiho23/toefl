# Part 1 — Structure (15 questions, 10 minutes)

Choose the option that best completes the sentence.

1. The results of the three experiments ___ published last month.
   (A) was  (B) were  (C) has been  (D) is
2. Neither the professor nor the students ___ aware of the change.
   (A) was  (B) were  (C) has been  (D) is being
3. A number of proposals ___ rejected by the committee.
   (A) was  (B) were  (C) has been  (D) is
4. By the time the delegation arrived, the ceremony ___ already begun.
   (A) has  (B) had  (C) was  (D) will have
5. She has been working on the manuscript ___ last April.
   (A) for  (B) since  (C) during  (D) from
6. The data must ___ before any conclusion is drawn.
   (A) verify  (B) be verified  (C) have verified  (D) verifying
7. The audience found the lecture thoroughly ___ .
   (A) bored  (B) boring  (C) bore  (D) boredom
8. The samples ___ in 2019 are still being analysed.
   (A) collecting  (B) collected  (C) were collected  (D) which collecting
9. It is essential that every applicant ___ the form by Friday.
   (A) submits  (B) submit  (C) submitted  (D) will submit
10. Could you tell me where ___ ?
    (A) is the library  (B) the library is  (C) the library  (D) does the library
11. Never ___ such a well-designed experiment.
    (A) we have seen  (B) have we seen  (C) we saw  (D) did we saw
12. The professor asked the students to read the chapter, take notes, and ___ .
    (A) to summarise it  (B) summarising it  (C) summarise it  (D) summarised
13. The reason the project was delayed is ___ the funding fell through.
    (A) because  (B) because of  (C) that  (D) due to
14. She is interested ___ marine biology.
    (A) on  (B) in  (C) at  (D) of
15. Her ___ surprised everyone in the department.
    (A) decisive  (B) decision  (C) decisively  (D) decide

# Part 2 — Reading (15 questions, 25 minutes)

## Passage A

> The domestication of the horse is one of the most consequential and least understood events
> in prehistory. Unlike cattle, sheep or pigs, horses are not obviously useful as food, and for
> several decades archaeologists assumed that they were first kept for meat and milk and only
> later ridden. That assumption is now in doubt.
>
> The evidence comes from an unexpected direction: the teeth. When a horse is controlled with a
> bit, the hard mouthpiece presses against a region of the lower premolar, and over years of use
> it produces a distinctive bevel — a slanted wear facet — that does not occur in wild animals.
> In 2009 a team examining Botai culture sites in northern Kazakhstan reported such bevels on
> several animals dated to roughly 3500 BC. Other researchers initially disputed the finding, but
> subsequent work using microscopic wear analysis has largely supported it.
>
> A second and independent line of evidence comes from milk. Lipid residues absorbed into
> pottery from the same sites indicate that mares were being milked, which implies close human
> control of breeding herds. And a third comes from genetics: modern domestic horses show
> dramatically greater diversity in their Y chromosomes than in their mitochondrial DNA, a pattern
> that is difficult to explain unless a relatively small number of stallions sired the offspring
> of many mares — precisely what happens when people, not nature, choose the mates.
>
> What remains genuinely unclear is what any of this meant for ordinary life. Riding transforms
> transport, herding and warfare, but the archaeological record preserves the horse long before it
> preserves the rider.

16. The passage is primarily concerned with
    (A) the nutritional value of horse milk
    (B) how and when horses were domesticated, and the evidence for it
    (C) the differences between wild and domestic horses
    (D) the military use of horses in prehistory
17. The word **consequential** in paragraph 1 is closest in meaning to
    (A) controversial  (B) significant  (C) recent  (D) unfortunate
18. According to paragraph 1, the earlier assumption about horses was that they were
    (A) ridden before they were eaten
    (B) never successfully domesticated
    (C) first kept for meat and milk
    (D) domesticated in southern Kazakhstan
19. The word **it** in paragraph 2 refers to
    (A) the bit  (B) the distinctive bevel  (C) the premolar  (D) the wild animal
20. What is a "bevel" as described in paragraph 2?
    (A) A type of bit used in Kazakhstan
    (B) A slanted wear facet on a horse's tooth caused by a bit
    (C) A chip in the enamel found only in wild horses
    (D) A surgical procedure performed on ancient horses
21. According to paragraph 3, lipid residues in pottery suggest that
    (A) horses were used for transport
    (B) mares were being milked
    (C) horses were wild
    (D) pottery was traded widely
22. The word **disputed** in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to
    (A) supported  (B) questioned  (C) discovered  (D) repeated
23. It can be inferred from the genetic evidence in paragraph 3 that
    (A) mares were more numerous in breeding than stallions
    (B) horses were never bred by humans
    (C) wild herds had greater diversity
    (D) domestication happened after 3500 BC

## Passage B

> Not every scientific advance is driven by new data. Some are driven by new bookkeeping.
>
> Consider the history of the calorie. The unit itself was defined in the nineteenth century as
> the amount of heat needed to raise the temperature of one gram of water by one degree Celsius,
> and for decades it belonged squarely to physics. What changed was not the measurement but the
> bookkeeping around it: the decision, taken early in the twentieth century, to treat the energy
> content of food as a property of the food rather than as a property of the person eating it.
>
> That shift had consequences nobody had planned. Once a food has a fixed number attached to it,
> diets can be calculated, rations can be standardised, and — crucially — the failure to lose
> weight can be reframed as arithmetic rather than physiology. The calorie made nutrition legible
> to bureaucracies, to armies, and eventually to consumers standing in a supermarket aisle. It
> also made invisible everything the unit cannot capture: the differing metabolic fates of
> identical quantities of fat, protein and carbohydrate, and the fact that the same food is
> digested differently depending on what it is eaten with.
>
> None of this makes the calorie wrong. It is a perfectly good measure of heat. The point is
> narrower and more interesting: a unit invented for steam engines became, through a series of
> administrative decisions, the lens through which an entire population learned to think about
> its own body.

24. What is the main idea of the passage?
    (A) The calorie is an inaccurate measure of energy.
    (B) A physics unit reshaped how nutrition was understood and managed, with unintended effects.
    (C) Armies were the first to calculate rations.
    (D) Fat, protein and carbohydrate are metabolised identically.
25. The word **legible** in paragraph 3 is closest in meaning to
    (A) readable  (B) comprehensible  (C) profitable  (D) invisible
26. According to the passage, the calorie was originally
    (A) a measure of nutritional value
    (B) a unit of heat used in physics
    (C) invented by nutritionists
    (D) based on the energy a person expends
27. The word **it** in paragraph 2 refers to
    (A) the calorie  (B) the measurement  (C) the food  (D) the person
28. According to paragraph 3, all of the following resulted from attaching a fixed number to food EXCEPT
    (A) diets could be calculated
    (B) rations could be standardised
    (C) the differing metabolic fates of nutrients became clearer
    (D) weight-loss failure could be reframed as arithmetic
29. What does the author say the calorie makes invisible?
    (A) The heat content of food
    (B) Differences in how nutrients are metabolised and how foods interact
    (C) The number of grams in a portion
    (D) The cost of food
30. Which best describes the author's attitude toward the calorie?
    (A) Hostile — it should be abolished
    (B) Measured — a good measure whose broader effects were unplanned
    (C) Uncritical — it solved nutrition completely
    (D) Confused
