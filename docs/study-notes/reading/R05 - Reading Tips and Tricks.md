# R05 — Reading Tips and Tricks

Legitimate test-taking shortcuts for the ITP Reading section. Read this before every timed passage.
Pairs with [R01 — Question Types](<R01 - Question Types.md>) and [R02 — Vocabulary in Context](<R02 - Vocabulary in Context.md>).

**The ITP Reading section:** 50 questions · about 5 passages · 55 minutes → **about 11 minutes per passage**.
There is **no penalty for wrong answers**, so never leave a blank.

---

## 1. The 5-step routine for every passage

| Step | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Skim | 1 min | Read the **first sentence of each paragraph** and the last sentence of the passage. You now know the map. |
| 2. Read the questions | 1 min | Read only the question stems (not the options). Note the paragraph or line each one points to. |
| 3. Read the passage | 3 min | Read at normal speed. Mark names, numbers, and words like *however, but, therefore*. |
| 4. Answer in order | 5 min | Questions follow the passage order, so work top to bottom. |
| 5. Check | 1 min | Fill every blank. Re-check only NOT/EXCEPT questions. |

If a question takes more than **1 minute**, guess, mark it, and move on.

---

## 2. Tricks by question type

### Main idea / "primarily about"
- The answer covers **the whole passage**, not one paragraph.
- Wrong answers are **too narrow** (only one detail) or **too broad** (goes beyond the text).
- Check: does the first sentence of every paragraph support this option?

### Detail / "according to the passage"
- Find the **paragraph or line** before you read the options.
- The correct answer is a **paraphrase**. An option that copies the passage word for word is often a trap.
- Wrong options reuse words from the passage but change the meaning.

### NOT / EXCEPT *(your weak spot)*
1. Cover the options and find the relevant paragraph.
2. Mark each option **T** (stated in the text) or **F** (not stated or contradicted).
3. Three will be T. The **F** is your answer.

Do not pick the first true statement you see. Read all four options.

### Vocabulary in context *(your weak spot)*
1. Ignore the word's usual meaning. Look at the **sentence around it**.
2. Check for a clue: a **dash**, **comma definition**, **"or"**, **"that is"**, or a contrast word (*but, whereas*).
3. **Substitute each option into the sentence.** Cross out the ones that sound wrong.
4. Common words are often used in a less common sense (*rival = equal*), so the usual meaning can be the trap.

### Pronoun reference
- The answer is almost always **before** the pronoun, usually in the same or the previous sentence.
- Plug your answer in place of the pronoun and re-read the sentence. It must make sense and agree in number (singular/plural).

### Inference
- The answer is **one small step** from the text, never a big leap.
- Look for "must be true" language. Avoid options with *always, never, all, completely*.

### Tone / attitude / purpose *(your weak spot)*
- Academic passages are usually **neutral, analytical, or cautious**.
- **Cross out extremes**: *hostile, dismissive, enthusiastic, outraged, uncritical*.
- Words like *arguably, may, some researchers* signal a cautious author.

### Why does the author mention X?
- Go to the sentence **before and after** X. The purpose is how it supports the author's point (an example, a contrast, a counterargument, an alternative).

---

## 3. Elimination rules

Cross out an option when it:

| Red flag | Example |
|---|---|
| Uses an extreme word | *always, never, only, all, none, completely* |
| Is true in real life but **not in the passage** | outside knowledge |
| Contradicts the passage even slightly | one reversed word |
| Is correct but answers a **different question** | wrong paragraph |
| Repeats the passage word for word on a vocabulary or inference question | usually a trap |

Eliminating two options turns a 25% guess into a 50% guess.

---

## 4. Signal words that tell you what comes next

| Signal | Meaning | Questions often ask about |
|---|---|---|
| however, but, yet, although, whereas | contrast | the author's real point is after this word |
| therefore, thus, consequently | result | cause and effect, inference |
| for example, such as, for instance | example | detail, "why mention X" |
| in fact, indeed, notably | emphasis | main idea, detail |
| first, second, finally | list | detail, NOT/EXCEPT |
| arguably, some say, critics claim | cautious or other view | tone, inference |

---

## 5. Speed tricks

1. **Don't read every word with equal attention.** Slow down at topic sentences and signal words, and speed up through examples.
2. **Don't re-read the passage for every question.** Use your skim map to go straight to the paragraph.
3. **Use the question order.** If Q3 is about paragraph 2 and Q5 is about paragraph 3, Q4 is almost surely between them.
4. **Skip unknown words** unless a vocabulary question asks about them. The sentence usually makes sense without them.
5. **Leave hard questions for last**, and fill in a guess first so you never leave a blank.

## 6. Common mistakes (from your own error log)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Picked a **true** statement on NOT/EXCEPT | Mark T/F on all four options |
| Chose a word's **usual meaning** on a vocabulary question | Substitute every option into the sentence |
| Picked an **extreme tone** option | Cross out extreme words; pick neutral or cautious |
| Answered from the wrong paragraph | Find the paragraph number **before** reading options |
