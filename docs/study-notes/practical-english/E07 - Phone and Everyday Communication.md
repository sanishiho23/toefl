# E07 — Phone and Everyday Communication

**Where it pays off:** Listening Part A (phone-style exchanges, message-taking, making plans)
and real-world work/study. Pairs with [E04 — Business & Workplace English](<E04 - Business and Workplace English.md>).
**Time to learn:** 20 min · **Target on the drill:** ≥ 12 / 15.

> Phone and messaging English is **fixed chunks** again. The audio rarely says things in a
> "creative" way — learn the formula and the answer is half-heard before it's spoken.

---

## 1. Starting and answering a call

| Function | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Answering (work) | *Good morning, [company], this is Sani speaking.* |
| Saying who you are | *Hi, it's Sani (calling) from…* |
| Asking for someone | *Could I speak to Mr Tan, please?* / *Is Maria there?* |
| Connecting | *Hold on, I'll put you through.* / *One moment, please.* |
| Not available | *I'm afraid she's not available / in a meeting right now.* |
| Wrong number | *Sorry, I think you've got the wrong number.* |

## 2. Taking and leaving a message

| Function | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Offer | *Can I take a message?* / *Would you like to leave a message?* |
| Leave one | *Could you tell him that Sani called?* / *Could you ask her to call me back?* |
| Call back | *I'll call back later.* / *What's the best time to reach you?* |
| Confirm | *Let me read that back to you…* / *So that's… , is that right?* |
| Numbers | spell and read digits one by one — see [E01 §4](<E01 - Numbers, Decimals and Quantities.md>) |

## 3. Phone call vs text message — register shift

| Spoken / formal (call, email) | Casual (text, chat) |
|---|---|
| *I'll be about ten minutes late.* | *running 10 late!* |
| *Could we reschedule to Thursday?* | *thurs instead?* |
| *Thank you, talk soon.* | *ty, ttyl* |
| *Please find the details below.* | *details below 👇* |

**Rule of thumb:** a **call or email** uses full sentences and *could/would*; a **text** can drop
subjects and use abbreviations. For the test and any formal writing, always use the left column.

## 4. Making arrangements & summarising action points

| Function | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Propose | *How about Tuesday at three?* / *Does Friday work for you?* |
| Agree | *That works.* / *Sounds good.* / *See you then.* |
| Change | *Something's come up — can we move it?* |
| Summarise | *So, to sum up: I'll send the file, you'll review it by Monday.* |
| Action points | *Action points: (1) I email the draft, (2) you confirm the room.* |
| Confirm back | *Just to confirm, we're meeting at the library at two.* |

## 5. Polite sign-offs

| Channel | Sign-off |
|---|---|
| Phone | *Thanks for calling. Talk soon. Bye.* |
| Formal email | *Kind regards,* / *Best regards,* |
| Semi-formal email | *Best,* / *Thanks,* |
| Text | *cheers / ttyl / 👍* |

## Exercise — 15 items, 8 minutes

**Choose the best phrase (1–10).**

1. Answering at work: "Good morning, this (is / am) Sani speaking."
2. "Could I (speak to / speak with to) Mr Tan, please?"
3. "Hold on, I'll (put you through / put through you)."
4. "I'm afraid she's (not available / no available) right now."
5. Offering: "Can I (take / make) a message?"
6. "Could you ask her to call me (back / again back)?"
7. Proposing a time: "Does Friday (work / working) for you?"
8. Summarising: "So, to (sum up / summary), I'll send the file."
9. Formal email ending: "(Kind regards / See ya),"
10. "Just to (confirm / confirmation), we're meeting at two."

**Rewrite the text message in full, spoken/formal form (11–15).**

12. "thurs instead?" →
13. "ty ttyl" →
