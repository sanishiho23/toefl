# S02 — Verb Tenses & Time Markers

**Section:** Structure & Written Expression · **Est. yield:** 3–5 questions
**Time to learn:** 25 min

---

## The one rule

> **Never choose a tense from intuition. Find the time marker first. It decides the answer.**

In Section 2, roughly one item in five is a tense question, and almost every one of them
contains an adverb, a date, or a conjunction that forces a single correct tense.

---

## Marker → tense lookup

| Marker in the sentence | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| yesterday, last ___, in 1998, ago, when + past | **simple past** | She *submitted* it yesterday. |
| now, currently, today, these days, always, generally | **simple present** | He *works* at the lab now. |
| tomorrow, next ___, soon, in two years | **simple future** | They *will announce* it next week. |
| since (point in time), for (duration), already, yet, ever, never, just, recently, so far | **present perfect** | She *has lived* here since 2019. |
| by the time + past, before + past, after + past (earlier of two past events) | **past perfect** | The train *had left* by the time we arrived. |
| by + future time, by the time + present | **future perfect** | By June he *will have finished*. |
| at 5 pm yesterday, while, when the phone rang | **past progressive** | I *was reading* when she called. |
| at this time tomorrow | **future progressive** | At noon I *will be flying*. |
| for two hours (up to now) | **present perfect progressive** | They *have been waiting* for two hours. |

## The five patterns that produce the most errors

### 1. `since` vs `for`
- **since** = a *point* in time → present perfect (*since 2010, since Monday, since she arrived*)
- **for** = a *duration* → present perfect (*for six years, for a long time*)
```
She has taught here ___ 2015.        → since
She has taught here ___ six years.   → for
```

### 2. `by the time` = the earliest event
```
By the time the results ___ , the semester had ended.
(A) publish  (B) were published  (C) had published  (D) will publish
```
Two past events. Publishing came first → **past perfect**? Careful: *by the time* + past
usually takes simple past in the subordinate clause, and the main clause takes past perfect.
Here the blank is the *by the time* clause → **B (were published)**.

### 3. `used to` vs `be used to`
- **used to + base verb** = a past habit that stopped → *He used to smoke.*
- **be used to + gerund/noun** = be accustomed to → *He is used to working late.*

### 4. Sequence with `after / before / when / until`
The earlier action takes the perfect; the later one takes the simple form.
```
After she ___ her thesis, she applied for the position.  → had finished
```

### 5. Present perfect never takes a finished past time
```
❌ I have seen him yesterday.   ✔ I saw him yesterday.
❌ She has graduated in 2020.   ✔ She graduated in 2020.
```
This is one of the most common Written Expression errors on the test.

## Voice: active vs passive

Passive = `be + past participle`. Choose passive when:
- the doer is unknown or unimportant, **OR**
- the subject *receives* the action.

```
The report ___ by the committee last week.   → was reviewed   (report receives)
The results of the survey ___ tomorrow.      → will be published
```
⚠️ Modals take `be + p.p.`: *must be submitted, should have been sent, can be found.*

## Exercise — 12 items, 7 minutes

1. By the time the delegation arrived, the meeting ___ already begun.
   (A) has  (B) had  (C) was  (D) will have
2. Professor Alvarez ___ at this university since 2012.
   (A) teaches  (B) taught  (C) has taught  (D) is teaching
3. The proposals ___ by the review board next Friday.
   (A) will consider  (B) will be considered  (C) will have considered  (D) are considering
4. She ___ in Paris in 1999, and she has not returned since.
   (A) lives  (B) has lived  (C) lived  (D) had lived
5. The students ___ for three hours when the fire alarm rang.
   (A) studied  (B) have studied  (C) had been studying  (D) are studying
6. All applications must ___ before the end of the month.
   (A) submit  (B) be submitted  (C) have submitted  (D) submitted
7. He used to ___ late, but now he is used to ___ early.
   (A) sleep / rise  (B) sleeping / rising  (C) sleep / rising  (D) sleeping / rise
8. By next December, they ___ the project for exactly two years.
   (A) will manage  (B) will have been managing  (C) will be managing  (D) have managed
9. The data ___ carefully before any conclusions were drawn.
   (A) was analyzed  (B) has analyzed  (C) analyzing  (D) were analyze
10. I ___ the report yet; I need another hour.
    (A) did not finish  (B) have not finished  (C) had not finished  (D) do not finish
11. When the power failed, the researchers ___ a delicate measurement.
    (A) performed  (B) were performing  (C) have performed  (D) had been performed
12. She has been working on the manuscript ___ last spring.
    (A) for  (B) since  (C) during  (D) from
