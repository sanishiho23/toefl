# S01 — Subject–Verb Agreement & Word Order

**Section:** Structure & Written Expression · **Est. yield:** 4–6 questions per test
**Time to learn:** 25 min · **Prerequisite:** none

---

## The one rule

> The verb agrees with the **headword of the subject** — not with the noun nearest to it.

TOEFL builds Section 2 questions by putting stuff between the subject and the verb so you
grab the wrong noun. Your job is mechanical: **delete the interruption, then look at the verb.**

---

## The eight decoys

### 1. Prepositional phrase between subject and verb
```
The results of the experiment ___ surprising.
```
Delete *of the experiment* → **The results … were**. ✔ `were`, not `was`.

> Also: *along with, as well as, together with, including, rather than, in addition to* —
> all interruptions. **The mayor, as well as his aides, is* here.

### 2. Inverted sentences (verb before subject)
```
There ___ several unanswered questions.            → are  (questions = subject)
On the wall ___ two large portraits.               → hang (portraits = subject)
Rarely ___ such dedication.                        → has one seen
```
`there is / there are` — look at what follows.

### 3. `one of the` vs `the only one of the`
```
One of the students ___ absent.                    → is   (one … is)
She is the only one of the students who ___ passed. → has  (the only one … has)
She is one of those students who ___ always early.  → are  (who → students)
```
The relative pronoun `who/that` refers to the **nearest preceding noun** — except after *the only one of*.

### 4. Gerund or infinitive as subject
```
Collecting stamps ___ his only hobby.              → is
To finish by Friday ___ impossible.                → is
```
Two subjects joined by *and* → plural. *Collecting coins and reading maps are*…

### 5. Quantity expressions — memorise these pairs

| Singular verb | Plural verb |
|---|---|
| the number of | a number of |
| the variety of | a variety of |
| the amount of | an amount of (uncountable) |
| each / every / either / neither / one | several / few / many / both |
| every student and teacher | students and teachers |
| anybody / everyone / nobody | people / those who |

```
The number of applicants ___ risen.                → has
A number of applicants ___ been rejected.          → have
Each of the answers ___ correct.                   → is
```

### 6. Collective nouns
TOEFL treats them as **singular units**: *committee, team, family, government, jury, faculty, staff, audience, class, group.*
```
The committee ___ reached a decision.              → has
```

### 7. Nouns that look plural but aren't (and vice versa)

| Singular (take *is/was*) | Plural (take *are/were*) |
|---|---|
| mathematics, physics, economics, ethics, statistics (as a field), athletics | criteria, phenomena, data, media, analyses, bacteria |
| news, measles, mathematics | goods, belongings, surroundings, scissors, pants |

### 8. Correlative pairs — agree with the **nearest** subject
```
Neither the teacher nor the students ___ ready.    → are  (students)
Neither the students nor the teacher ___ ready.    → is   (teacher)
Not only the students but also their professor ___ invited. → was
```

### Bonus trap: one person or two?
```
The professor and the dean ___ attending.          → are  (two people — two articles)
The professor and dean ___ attending.              → is   (one person — one article)
```

## Word-order rules that ride along

| Rule | Right | Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| adjective before noun | a *beautiful old* painting | a painting beautiful |
| adjective order: opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material | a *small round wooden* table | a wooden round small table |
| `enough` after adjective, before noun | old *enough* / *enough* time | enough old / time enough |
| adverb of frequency: after *be*, before other verbs | she *is always* late / she *always arrives* | she always is |
| `too … to` | too *difficult to* solve | too difficult for solve |
| `so … that` | so *heavy that* it broke | so heavy so it broke |

## Exercise — 12 items, 7 minutes

Choose the correct form. Answers at the bottom — don't peek.

1. The effects of the new policy on small businesses ___ still unknown.
   (A) is  (B) are  (C) has been  (D) was
2. Neither the students nor their instructor ___ satisfied with the results.
   (A) are  (B) were  (C) is  (D) have been
3. A number of proposals ___ submitted before the deadline.
   (A) was  (B) has been  (C) were  (D) has
4. There ___ several reasons why the experiment failed.
   (A) is  (B) are  (C) has been  (D) was
5. Mathematics ___ always been considered a difficult subject.
   (A) have  (B) are  (C) has  (D) were
6. The professor and dean of the college ___ resigning next month.
   (A) are  (B) is  (C) were  (D) have
7. On the desk ___ a notebook and two pens.
   (A) is  (B) was  (C) are  (D) has been
8. She is one of those researchers who ___ never missed a deadline.
   (A) has  (B) have  (C) is  (D) was
9. Collecting rare manuscripts ___ become increasingly expensive.
   (A) have  (B) are  (C) has  (D) were
10. The committee ___ its final report yesterday.
    (A) submit  (B) have submitted  (C) submits  (D) submitted
11. The news about the scholarship recipients ___ encouraging.
    (A) are  (B) were  (C) is  (D) have been
12. Each of the participants ___ required to sign a consent form.
    (A) are  (B) were  (C) is  (D) have been
