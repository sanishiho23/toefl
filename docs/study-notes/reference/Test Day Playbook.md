# Test-Day Playbook

Read this the night before. Read it again in the car.

---

## The night before

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lay out ID, admission ticket, 3 sharpened pencils, eraser, analogue watch | Try a new "last-minute" grammar rule |
| Eat a normal dinner | Pull an all-nighter — you lose more from fatigue than you gain from study |
| Sleep 8 hours | Drink alcohol "to relax" |
| Check the route and travel time | Scroll social media until 2 a.m. |

---

## The morning

- **Wake 2.5–3 hours before** the test. Your brain is not fully online for the first hour.
- **Eat protein, not sugar.** Eggs, yoghurt, oatmeal. A sugar spike crashes you at minute 60 —
  right in the middle of Reading.
- **Do one easy warm-up.** 10 flashcards, one easy passage. Something you *know*.
  The goal is to feel fluent, not to learn.
- **Arrive 30 minutes early.** Rushing into the room elevates cortisol, which destroys listening.

---

## In the room

- Sit where you can see the clock or bring your **analogue** watch (smartwatches are usually banned).
- Keep water within reach.
- Put your phone **off** and in your bag. Not silent. Off.

---

## Section 1 — Listening (50 Q, 35 min)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Audio plays **once** | There is no rewind. Ever. |
| **Look ahead is impossible** — you can't see the questions | So your only job is to *understand* |
| Take notes on numbers, names, contrast markers | Free questions |
| **Let go instantly** | Chasing a missed item costs you three more |
| **Never change an answer** | First instinct on audio beats second-guessing |
| Transfer answers during the stated time only | Proctors will not warn you twice |

**Emergency move:** if you completely lose a conversation, pick the option that is *least
extreme* and move on. Extreme options (*impossible, never, always, furious*) are wrong ~80% of the time.

---

## Section 2 — Structure (40 Q, 25 min)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **37 seconds per question** | Faster than any other section |
| Read the whole sentence first | Half the errors are visible only at the end |
| Answer the *slot*, not the word | Article → noun · *very/more* → adjective · action → verb |
| **Blank = guess now, move on** | 25 unanswered questions is the classic disaster |
| Questions ~16–40 are error-finding | Read each underlined word individually |
| Watch the clock at 12 min | You must be past question 20 |

**Error-finding shortcut:** if four underlined words look fine, the answer is usually a
**word form** (S08) or a **preposition**. Check those two first.

---

## Section 3 — Reading (50 Q, 55 min)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **66 seconds per question** | Roughly 11 minutes per passage |
| Read the passage once, properly | Skimming costs more in re-reading than it saves |
| Attack in the [question-type order](<../reading/[19-Oct] R01 - Question Types.md>) | Cheap points first |
| Write "NOT" big when you see *EXCEPT* | Cheapest lost points on the test |
| Watch the clock at 30 min | You must be past question 25 |
| **Never leave a bubble empty** | No guessing penalty |

**If you're running out of time:** do main-idea questions first — they're answerable from the
first and last sentences without re-reading. Then guess the rest.

---

## The universal rules

1. **There is no guessing penalty.** An empty bubble is 0. A guess is 25%. Never leave one blank.
2. **Never change an answer unless you found a specific reason.** Gut changes are net-negative.
3. **Watch the clock, not other people.** Someone finishing early means nothing.
4. **One hard question is worth the same as one easy question.** Don't spend 3 minutes on either.

---

## After

- Don't debrief with other test takers. It serves nothing and it stresses you.
- If you have a retake window, note **while it's fresh**: which section felt worst, which
  question types you saw most. Put that in `docs/logs/daily/`.
- Then close the repo for a week. Rest is part of the process.
