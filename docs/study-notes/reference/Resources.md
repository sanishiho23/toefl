## Audio for Listening practice

Because TOEFL ITP audio plays **once**, you need material you can play once and never replay.

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| ETS official samples (above) | Highest fidelity to the real test |
| [L04 script bank](<../listening/[24-Oct] L04 - Audio Script Bank.md>) in this repo | Feed to any TTS app at ~150 wpm |
| Your phone's built-in screen reader / TTS | Easiest zero-setup option for scripts |
| VOA Learning English | Slower, clear American English — good for building stamina early |
| BBC Learning English / 6 Minute English | British accents, good for accent flexibility |
| TED-Ed | Lecture-shaped, close to Part C structure |

⚠️ **Do not use podcasts with music or multiple fast speakers.** They train a skill the test
does not measure.

## Dictionaries — use an English–English one

| Dictionary | Why |
|---|---|
| Cambridge (dictionary.cambridge.org) | Clear definitions, British & American pronunciation |
| Longman (ldoceonline.com) | Best for learners; shows collocations and word families |
| Oxford Learner's (oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com) | Good for academic register labels |

> ⚠️ **Stop using a bilingual dictionary from Week 2 onward.** Vocabulary-in-context questions
> require you to feel the *register* of a word, and translation flattens that.

## What NOT to buy

- Anything promising "TOEFL ITP past papers". The ITP is an *institutional* test; retired forms
  are not officially published. Anything sold as a "real past paper" is reconstructed.
- Apps that only drill vocabulary and never time you.
- Anything that teaches Speaking or Writing — **Level 1 in its standard form has neither.**
  Don't spend a minute on them unless your institution administers the Speaking add-on.

Two things you must confirm with your institution, because they vary:

1. **Paper or digital?** (Affects whether you can write on the test book.)
2. **Which level?** Level 1 or Level 2 — they are not interchangeable.
